# Consultation Response Drafter

Regulators read hundreds of responses. The ones that move policy are specific, evidenced, aligned to the regulator's stated objectives, and offer workable alternative drafting. This skill produces that.

## Operating standard

- Read the full consultation, its annexes, draft rules/instruments and any linked feedback statements before drafting. Never answer from the question list alone.
- Each proposition is labelled: **(F)** fact/evidence, **(L)** legal text, **(P)** policy argument. Unsupported assertions are not allowed.
- No confidential business information goes into a draft without the user confirming it can be disclosed. Regulators may publish responses; flag what should go in a confidential annex.

## Step 1 — Intake and scoping

Capture: consultation reference, issuing body, deadline and submission method/format (response form, online portal, email; word limits), whether responses are published, the respondent (company, trade body, joint), and the respondent's business model touchpoints. Identify the regulator's statutory objectives and the stated purpose of the consultation — every argument should connect to one of them (e.g. consumer protection, market integrity, competition, financial stability, proportionality).

## Step 2 — Question map

Table: Q# | Topic | Proposal in the paper (with paragraph/rule reference) | Impact on respondent (High/Med/Low/None) | Position (Support / Support with amendments / Oppose / No view) | Owner | Evidence needed. Recommend which questions to skip — a focused response is stronger.

## Step 3 — Position matrix and strategy

For each High/Medium question: the regulator's objective; the respondent's position; the strongest counter-argument the regulator will raise; the evidence that answers it; the **alternative proposal** (drafting or mechanism) that achieves the objective with less cost. Check consistency across answers and with the respondent's public positions and past submissions.

Comparative angle where useful: how the same issue is handled in other regimes (e.g. EU MiCA vs UK cryptoasset regime; IOSCO/FSB recommendations) — verify each comparator from primary sources.

## Step 4 — Evidence requests

Generate a list for the business: data points (volumes, user numbers, cost estimates for implementation, incident data), case studies, technical explanations. Mark each as public/confidential.

## Step 5 — Draft responses

Per question: (1) one-line answer; (2) reasoning tied to the regulator's objective; (3) evidence; (4) proposed amendment — in the regulator's drafting style (e.g. "We propose that rule X.Y be amended as follows: …"), with tracked-style insertions/deletions where helpful; (5) implementation/transitional points. Keep a neutral, constructive tone; avoid adversarial language and lobbying clichés.

## Step 6 — Wrap-around documents

- Executive summary (≤ 1 page) with top 3–5 asks.
- Cover letter/email to the regulator.
- Confidential annex (if any).
- Internal briefing note: positions, risks of each ask, what success looks like, follow-up engagement (roundtables, bilateral meetings).

## Quality gate before release

- Every legal reference checked against the current text.
- Answers consistent with each other and with the respondent's other regulatory filings.
- Confidentiality review done; publishable version separated.
- Word limits and format met; deadline and submission channel confirmed.

## Output

Question map → position matrix → evidence list → full draft response in the regulator's format → executive summary → cover note → internal briefing.
